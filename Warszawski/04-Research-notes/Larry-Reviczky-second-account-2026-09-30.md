# Larry's bread deliveries: a second published account
September 30, 2026. Research note for the Warszawski/Hersh side.

## Finding and evidence level
Adam Reviczky's *Vesztes háborúk - megnyert csaták. Emlékezés Reviczky Imre ezredesre* contains testimony attributed to "Larry Hirsch". The speaker places his childhood home in Huszt, says he was born in1925, describes speaking Hungarian/German/Czech/Slovak/Russian, and recounts a forged summons to Nagybanya paid for by his father. These distinctive details closely match Larry Hersh's USHMM interview. The book explicitly names Lieutenant Colonel Reviczky as the commander who permitted his bread deliveries.

This is a strong identity match and a second published version of Larry's recollections, not independent documentary proof of every event. The publisher's readable OCR was recovered; the original page images were subscription-gated and have NOT been visually verified. Hungarian wording may have OCR errors. No purchase was made. Do not substitute this text for an inspected scan when exact quotation is required.

A bookseller lists a1985 Magvető edition, Budapest,776pp. The text researched here is the2008 Múlt és Jövő edition, printed448-450, digital469-471 (viewer displays470-472). The1985 listing establishes an edition, not the first publication date or proof that its text is identical.

## Cautious working translation of the Larry passage
Printed448: The author introduces "Larry Hirsch" among forceful young people and calls him a Zionist. Larry says Joel Brand went from Budapest to Istanbul and Palestine in1944; he had been in contact with him from age17 while living with his parents in Huszt. They hoped someone from Palestine would come to help, though they did not know how. Speaking several languages and dressed in boots and a green hat, he could travel without outsiders recognizing him as Jewish. After the Germans arrived, his father, sensing his involvement, paid a clerk to forge a summons to Nagybanya. He says he escaped there in April1944 before the ghetto, was accepted into the barracks, received food and a place, and initially hoped to reach Budapest.

Printed449: Realizing he did not know whether his Budapest contacts still existed, he decided to do something useful locally. With a fellow sufferer from Huszt and another from the surrounding area, he collected bread offered from the labor servicemen's rations and considered buying more. He requested an audience with the commander. Lieutenant Colonel Reviczky listened and rebuked his assumption that help must be secret: the bread should be put on horse-drawn transport and delivered before it dried out. Larry says he learned to respect the commander. Soldiers and town police initially did not obstruct the deliveries; on one occasion gendarmes arrived while the group was inside and arrested them. Reviczky had difficulty getting them released.

Printed450: Larry says he no longer felt safe in the barracks, hid with Christian acquaintances, and went from Transylvania into the Great Plain. He could not escape his fate and was deported to Auschwitz. The author then comments on Larry's hope for help from Palestine and Joel Brand. The commentary is the author's, not Larry's testimony, and is not independently assessed here.

## Conflicts to preserve
1. USHMM part1 spells the humane commander "Kelety" and says the son was writing a book. This book names Reviczky explicitly in the Larry passage. Do not silently normalize the transcript name.
2. Book says April1944 forged summons and subsequent Nagybanya ghetto work. Oral transcript has1942/1943 references and calls the summons legitimate but obtained through payment. Independent USHMM Khust contextual history places its ghetto/deportations in1944.
3. Oral part2 describes bread hidden under hay, reported after departure, followed by the commander's quiet approval. Book describes an audience before transport and the commander's explicit permission. These are different accounts; do not blend them into one seamless scene.
4. Book says hiding with Christian acquaintances and travel into the Great Plain before deportation. Oral part2 describes returning to the ghetto after a failed bread run and being deported the next morning. Preserve both sequences.
5. Camp number in oral OCR is8485 in one passage and845 in another. No camp number is confirmed by this book passage.
