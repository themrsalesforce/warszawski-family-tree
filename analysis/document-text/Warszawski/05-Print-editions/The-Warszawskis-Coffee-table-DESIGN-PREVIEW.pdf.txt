KHUST      /   ISRAEL    /   CLEVELAND




Larry remembers
A life told in his own voice


In August 1984, Larry Hersh sat down with interviewer Peggy Nathan and told the story of
his life. He began in Khust, the town in Subcarpathia where he had grown up: school,
summer journeys into the Carpathians, a father who traded in lumber and wine. He
remembered reading Gone with the Wind in four languages.

His account carried him through the Holocaust and its aftermath, through the work of
helping Jews cross postwar borders, and on to Israel. These were memories he chose to put
on record. Where later tellings differ, the family history keeps those differences visible.

Larry dated his arrival in Cleveland to 1956. His sister was already there. He met Ileen
Garson, known in the family as Gabby, and together they raised three daughters: Lori,
Susanne and Marcy. In the family, the man whose testimony crossed so many places was
simply Zeidy Larry.

CLEVELAND        /   1988




A daughter is born
Ilana Chava Warszawski




On 26 August 1988, the Cleveland Jewish News carried a short announcement from
Joseph and Laurel Warszawski: their daughter Ilana Chava had been born on 4 June.



A few lines brought four generations onto one small piece of newsprint. Szymon and Berta
Warszawski were named as paternal grandparents; Lawrence and Ilene Hersh as maternal
grandparents. Mr. and Mrs. Mordechai Zelkind appeared as great-grandparents. The
newspaper’s spellings belong to the record.


Ilana was the first of Joe and Lori’s nine children to marry. Her own household would carry
the family story from Cleveland to Houston.




Original birth notice: Cleveland Jewish News, 26 August 1988, printed page 40. Archive crop preserved without alteration.
Family recollections and wedding announcement supply the later account.
