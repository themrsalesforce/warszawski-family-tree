## Highest-return changes

### 1. Explain a fact once, then make it easy to find

The same person can appear in narrative, a chart, a register, the household list, story notes and a catalogue. Each form can serve a different purpose, but it does not need to repeat the same biography or a long undifferentiated citation list.

Use the narrative for the story and the source's meaning. Use charts for relationships. Use register entries for aliases, compact dates with provenance, qualified relationship fields and short evidence notes. Use the catalogue for the full archival locator. Where a register entry already ends its paragraph with `[S...]` and immediately repeats the same list as `Sources: S...`, retain one citation treatment.

For Joe and Larry, the broad citation lists on pages 100–101 are especially poor substitutes for knowing which document supports which date. Replace the long list with a few claim-level references. This is both a space saving and an accuracy improvement.

### 2. Preserve all people without giving every short entry a biography

The current register has 183 entries, of which 92 are explicitly unlinked research people. Some short entries simply repeat a child's name, parents and the statement that the civil record documents that relationship. These can become compact household tables with columns for name, date, relationship, evidence and uncertainty.

Retain full entries for complex identities, conflicting records and direct ancestors. Keep name aliases searchable. Do not equate the 183 entries with 183 distinct proven relatives: the register includes unresolved named/unnamed identities, candidates and provisionally indexed ancestors.

Page 151 has one very short Yitzchak Reiter entry. Pages 164 and 235 also end with substantial unused space. Keep short entries together and allow adjacent material to flow; avoid creating a new page for every small leftover group.

### 3. Make the household section do something the register cannot

Pages 152–164 repeat many relationships already in the register and charts. Some groups have no supplied children. In the modest plan, keep a compact household table, explicit uncertainty flags and the explanation that an empty child list does not prove childlessness. Remove repeated narratives and sprawling source lists. Include a household ID for a stable cross-reference.

Keep the useful distinctions already present: co-parent group versus documented marriage; inferred grandchild allocation; indexed parentage unconfirmed. These distinctions must survive condensation. Also carry them into the register's otherwise flat parent/child fields.

### 4. Consolidate source notes while retaining image credits

There are 15 pages of story notes and 25 pages of original source catalogue, followed by four more pages of W sources. The proposed reduction is conservative because this is a substantial evidence base.

Give each source one canonical entry. Use a short story/image note containing only the relevant source ID, the exact claim or crop and any reproduction credit. Merge alternate locators for the same underlying document rather than treating them as independent evidence. For example, W01 and S063 describe the same marriage, while W02/W03 clarify Victor's naturalization evidence formerly discussed through Pearl's S070 petition. Keep their distinctions in a crosswalk, not parallel repeated biographies.

Generic viewer links should gain issue date, printed page, digital page/image, record number and local filename. Removing a duplicate catalogue entry is acceptable only when its locator and evidence scope remain available.

### 5. Integrate the later discoveries

The 24-page appendix on pages 205–228 documents important findings, but leaves older narrative/questions stale. Put the established 1924 name change, Pearl's delayed birth and Rose's sister affidavit into the Garson/Adelstein story. Retain a short appendix for the remaining candidate/conflict analysis and archival locators.

Pages 208–210 show the naturalization sequence through multiple document presentations. Test one readable original plus a detail inset and a concise timeline. Pages 211–214 can bring Pearl's birth, Rose's affidavit and Rose's marriage into a coherent two- or three-page evidence spread. Use the critical image areas at a size that can actually be read.

The French act and passenger manifest conflict discussion on pages 218–220 must not be condensed into “Victor's birth and immigration records.” The correct heading remains “candidate records under comparison.”

## Sample treatment, for a future working copy

**Victor's name and the next question**

Victor reported a Paris birth on 30 May 1895 in his American naturalization records. His 1924 court papers identify the earlier surname Gershanovitz and document the change to Garson. His 1916 marriage names parents Solomon and Rachael, without establishing that their surname was Garson. A Paris act for Victor Jerchonossitch matches the birth date, but names Israël and Rosa Pilensky as parents. A passenger record for Rachel and young Victor Gerschenowitz is also under comparison. These are promising candidates; neither has yet been securely joined to the Cleveland family. [W01–W03, W08–W10, W22]

This replaces repeated biographical framing while preserving the decisive conflict. It must not replace the individual record transcriptions, document images or complete source locators in the research archive.

## An optional larger reduction

A 150–175-page family edition is plausible if the detailed unlinked French, Kogen and other candidate research, their full registers and many search-negative discussions move into a separate research companion. Keep a two- to four-page “questions still open” summary in the family volume and cross-reference the companion by stable item IDs.

That option would improve the family reader's path considerably, but it changes the book's scope. Begin with the modest 194-page plan unless a clearly separate family/readership edition is wanted. Preserve the full research archive either way.

## Rules for a successful rebuild

- Preserve body and chart legibility. Do not achieve the target by reducing all text or placing full civil records at unreadable thumbnail size.
- Do not cut an uncertainty label merely because its explanation appears elsewhere. The qualifier belongs beside the relationship/date being read.
- Generate the table of contents, index, chart references, story notes and source links from the final layout.
- Keep original record images and transcriptions outside the layout so that future revisions can revisit the evidence.
- After rebuilding, compare all person/household entries and claim-level sources against this baseline, render every page and inspect a physical printer proof.
