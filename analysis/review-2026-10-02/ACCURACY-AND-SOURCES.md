## Certainty must survive outside the paragraph

**Indexed parents versus an established pedigree.** The chart on page 56 and the household entries on 161–162 flag Szyman's and Elka's indexed parentage as provisional. The register's flat parent/child fields on 108–109 and 138–139 read more definitively. Add the same qualification directly to those fields. The proposed Zisl/Joseph equivalence is not settled merely by transliteration. Public trees that copied the index are not independent confirmation.

**Inferred grandchild allocation.** The household list correctly labels the Komisarchik/Elin allocations as inferred. Pages 139–141 give flat children/parents lines before explaining that inference in prose. The Koby/Dylan allocation on 143 and household on 155 has a similar issue. Keep “inferred from grouped notice surnames” beside the relevant edges, especially in an export or diagram read without its note.

**Approximate births.** Julia's register field on 112 says 1877 while the narrative derives an approximate birth from her ages. Use approximately 1877, with the age-derived basis. Marius Gabriel's 1864 field on 115 should be distinguished from a birth-act identification; the book itself says his possible identity with Auguste is unproved. Do not merge the two men because their inferred ages agree.

**Death, burial, services and publication.** Larry's 3 January 2012 register date on 101 is more categorical than the narrative's explanation of compiled date and cemetery interment. Retain the source attribution and obtain an original obituary/certificate before representing the day as independently proved. Ileen's 22 May 2009 day has stronger obituary support than the old generic question suggests, but her certificate remains unexamined. For Esther Kogen, Leo/Ida Garson and Rosamond, do not turn a notice/publication month into an exact death date. The book already explains some of these differences well; retain those explanations in the fields too.

**Unresolved children are not additional children.** Pages 105–106 explicitly say the unnamed 2020 baby is not counted as an additional named child. Preserve that safeguard. Similarly, Arye/Leo and Rochelle's three grandsons are unresolved identity entries, not grounds for assigning extra children or inventing parentage. The Nesya/2025 announcement question remains an identity match, not merely a missing date.

**Family information is evidence with a named origin.** The latest children's names, nicknames and household rosters often come from S001/S030/S105. Keep that provenance. A wedding announcement or grandfather's obituary should not acquire responsibility for every later child in the same entry. “No spouse recorded” is preferable to asserting a present-day unmarried status from an old account. Joe's precise Polish birth city and Larry's father's given name and three lost sisters' names remain direct-family gaps worth closing before extending unrelated candidate trees.

## What the October 2 appendix has already established

These are findings already present in the imported current PDF, not newly discovered by this review:

- Victor's 1919 declaration and 1924 court papers document a Paris birth claim and the Gershanovitz-to-Garson name change. His parents' own surnames remain unestablished.
- Pearl's 1940 delayed birth material documents a Cleveland birth claim of 1 December 1897 and parents Sam and Rebecca Adelstein. Its filing date is 1940, not 1897; the sister affidavit supplies a different informant for the delayed record.
- Rose Alliance's 1940 affidavit calls her Pearl's sister. Rose's 1906 marriage names her mother Rebecca Escowitch. The CPL Rose Karr transcription connects the later Karr and earlier Alliance names; an original newspaper scan would strengthen that last link.
- Edward's veterans' card links Isidor Adelstein/Adelstein variant to Edward Allen. His 1952 marriage is additional evidence, with its own date and parentage scope. The notary Harry Allen in Pearl's affidavit is not proved to be her brother merely by a shared name.
- The 1920 household records Harry and Louis as Victor's brothers-in-law. This supports the household relationship, not automatically every brother's exact birthplace, parents or name-change history.

Keep original spellings alongside normalized identities. Each record proves a limited proposition. A family of Allen siblings can be reconstructed without pretending that all individual birth or surname-change records have been recovered.

The next useful record is a parent-identifying American original or a French/immigration chain that resolves both parent-name conflicts and follows the candidate into the Cleveland family. Another birth matching only name/date would not close that bridge.

## Citation and editorial repair

All referenced S identifiers have a catalogue definition. That mechanical check does not prove the citations are correct. The S019/S024 error illustrates why claim-level checking still matters.

Create a source crosswalk for the older S series and later W series. Record the underlying document, repository/collection, date type, record and image number, exact URL, local original, informant and supported claims. Two IDs or several webpages may refer to one document. Generic archive homepages and public family-tree profiles are finding aids, not substitutes for original records.

The notes on 173 still include the legacy title “The family at 770” beside the Sunday-dollars material; the “Where the proven Garson line stops” entry has no source explanation. Align notes with the actual current content. Combine the separate appendix index on 228 with the main index on 229–235 and add familiar aliases such as Abba, Grammy, Gabby and Zeidy/Zaida Larry. Index entries should lead to the story and relevant evidence as well as the register.

## Print evidence and technical limits

The print interior was checked directly, independently of the reading-copy compression. Large image placements on **pages 1, 6, 8, 13, 15, 23, 24, 28, 36, 43 and 92** are below 130 effective dpi under the audit's size threshold. Page 1 contains two placements of the same low-resolution asset. This is a triage measurement, not a universal printer acceptance rule. The original source quality may be the limiting factor. Recover better originals where possible; otherwise reproduce smaller with readable transcription or acknowledge the source limitation.

No extracted text span extends beyond the print page, and no inspected internal link target falls outside the document. The reading audit flags 24 overlapping text pairs on chart pages 50–61; they are duplicate running footers, not demonstrated body-text collisions. Many small-text flags concern running headings. Contact-sheet inspection cannot certify every tiny label or halftone in a physical book.

JEM previews on 48–49 still require the final chosen asset and reproduction permission status to be settled. Do not use the old 212-page image checklist as clearance for the current file. A physical proof remains a separate production step.

## Evidence files and reproducibility

The [retrieval log](source-verification/verification-log.json) records byte counts, SHA-256 checksums, URLs and failures. An HTTP 200 alone is not counted as a verified genealogical claim. Source snapshots are retained locally; PDF/image originals follow the repository's binary-ignore policy. [Assessment](source-verification/ASSESSMENT.md) records what was actually readable.

The actual edition audit is reproducible with `scripts/audit_review.py`; rendered reading contact sheets use `scripts/review_manuscript_layout.py`; targeted print checks use `scripts/audit_print_layout.py`. The dependencies are recorded in `scripts/requirements-review.txt`. External access can change, so future retrieval results belong in a new dated snapshot rather than replacing this review's historical log.
